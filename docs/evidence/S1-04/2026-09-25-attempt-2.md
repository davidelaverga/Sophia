# S1-04 — attempt 2 (2026-09-25): the Studio's visual language, camera and screen

**Level:** browser (real Studio, API, PostgreSQL 16 and the local LiveKit server v1.13.7; synthetic HS256 identities).
**Owner:** Luis · **Branch:** `studio/vision` (on `s1-04/studio-shell`) · **Machine:** Windows 11, Node 24.21.0, pnpm 11.7.0.
**Goal status:** `in_progress`. As in attempt 1, two people actually talking needs microphones and a LiveKit server both founders reach.

## What changed

1. **One visual language across the Studio**, from the approved prototype of Sophia's light.
   - A dark room lit by Sophia: people are warm light, she is violet.
   - Instrument Sans for the interface. Instrument Serif for titles and, in italic, for Sophia's own line.
   - Hairlines instead of boxes, film grain, and one easing curve.
   - Sign-in, loading and the project home are quiet rooms with her light at rest.
   - Goals and Work read like an editorial page.
2. **Sophia's light as a component** (`apps/studio/src/features/light/`). A WebGL shader, composited with `screen` so no edge ever shows; a 2D trace; critically damped springs.
   - **Real state only:**
     - she rests before you join and listens once you are in;
     - she leans toward whoever holds the input floor;
     - a line travels the room's edge while goals run.
   - **The floor's path:** when the floor changes hands, it travels from one person, through her, to the next. The person who passes sees it leave on the receipt; the others see it when the room record changes.
   - **Degrades gracefully:**
     - under load, the resolution steps down;
     - without WebGL, a CSS glow replaces it;
     - with reduced motion, it holds still.
3. **The room is the Studio view.** People sit around the light, and one floating dock holds microphone, camera, screen, the floor and leave. The connection lives in the project shell, so reading Goals or Work keeps you in the room, with a mini dock there.
4. **Camera and screen share (LiveKit).** The room token now lets editors and admins publish camera and screen (with its sound) as well as the microphone; viewers still only listen and watch.
   - Cameras make a gallery of 16:10 tiles, sized to fit and centered like a table.
   - A shared screen takes the stage, with the tiles beside it.
   - Sophia keeps a transparent tile, and her light moves into it.
   - Tiles keep the same video element while a track lives; your own camera is mirrored.
