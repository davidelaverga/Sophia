# Aesthetic routing

Read this for a new direction, a full rework, or when the person explicitly cares about the visual direction. The goal is not a fashionable style label but an executable visual system derived from the subject, the readers, the medium and real benchmarks. [A0.1]

## 1. Lock the evidence authority [A1.1–A1.2]

Record only evidence that changes composition, type, colour range or base element language, one line each: `evidence_id / source_type / observed_claim / artifact / supported_axes / unsupported_axes`. `source_type` is one of `USER_CURRENT`, `USER_ACCEPTED_REFERENCE`, `BRAND_OR_SYSTEM`, `OBSERVED_PRODUCTION_REFERENCE` (a bundled precedent page you actually viewed), `AUTHORITATIVE_CONSTRAINT` (the frozen package, the static profile), `MODEL_HYPOTHESIS`.

Your brief, direction, topic associations and decision card are `MODEL_HYPOTHESIS`: they form candidates and a verification plan only. No evidence crosses its `supported_axes`: the subject does not decide the global colour; a platform rule does not decide the tone. Only references the person currently accepted and that you can actually view form protected invariants.

## 2. Read the task and its world [A2.1–A2.3]

Take the visual material from the person's request, the authoritative material and the real content, not from a model-made brief or "common web templates":

- **people:** who reads it, their visual literacy, language and trust needs;
- **action:** reading, comparing, judging, looking up;
- **objects:** the real tools, materials, documents, measurements and traces of the subject;
- **context:** what tone public service, consumer, culture, science, industry or education allow;
- **time:** a one-off read, continuous reading or repeated reference need different rhythms;
- **limits:** language, target widths, data credibility and accessibility veto some directions;
- **aesthetic memory:** references the person gave now, and what they accepted, rejected or repeatedly pointed at; the current request comes first.

Extract enough concrete nouns and actions to distinguish this report. If all that remains is "platform, data, intelligent, efficient, explore", or the words cannot affect composition, content and acceptance, the subject is not understood yet. For repeated collaboration write two more lines: what to keep, and which habit explicitly not to repeat.

## 3. Choose real benchmarks [A3.1–A3.4]

**Adapted:** Raven browses for production benchmarks. Here the benchmarks are what you can actually view: admitted references, and the bundled precedent pages (`web/precedents/page-01`, `page-04`, `page-06`). Choose the smallest set that covers the risk; stop when another one would not change a decision. Never invent a URL, year, mechanism or production evidence from memory; when a key risk has no viewable evidence, record the gap.

Reject choosing by palette similarity, award labels, a single hero frame, or "it looks like Apple, Linear or Stripe". For each benchmark record: `fact_id / reference_id / name / frame (page) / what is the same (task, medium, readers, density) / observed_relation / protected_axis / supported_axes / free_axes / non_transferable / target_object / representative_frame_evidence / final_frame_evidence / deviation_constraint`. Without an actual frame it is not a visual benchmark.

When a reference is the main visual basis these fields form the reference contract: three to six observable relationships, at least one on structure or reading order, one on hierarchy or density, one on the main object; only colour, adjectives or a name do not complete it. What transfers is the semantic relationship, not literal coordinates.

## 4. Extract mechanisms [A4.1–A4.2]

Do not extract surface results ("black and white, huge titles, much white space"). Write observations as causes: "the opening shows one main action, so the reader does not choose between story and task"; "a narrow caption column fixes image–text ownership while the body keeps a continuous measure"; "the same object keeps the same colour in list, table and detail". Each mechanism must become code, content structure or an acceptance action; adjectives that cannot do not enter the decision card. Also record what does not transfer: unique brand symbols, proprietary type, photography, copy and data.

## 5. Route the visual family [A5.1]

A research report's main family is **editorial and documentary** (author order, evidence in text and tables, reading rhythm, caption ownership; its usual mistake is beige paper, big serif titles and hairline newspaper sets) and, when the core is comparison, **data journalism and analysis** (comparison-led, annotation, scale, source and narrative order; its usual mistake is putting every number in a KPI card). Other families (institutional trust, product and tool, technical instrument, brand and culture, teaching, games) apply only when the content really needs them, with their job written down. Names such as Apple, GOV.UK or the New York Times may only name an observable mechanism: write what to learn, never "make it like that".

## 6. Decide the relevant axes [A6.1–A6.2]

Questions that expose trade-offs, not a form to fill. Record only axes that change implementation or acceptance, choosing in words with a one-line task reason each: **order** (strict rules / controlled asymmetry / free scene), **density** (sparse focus / medium narrative / dense work), **contrast** (quiet continuity / clear hierarchy / dramatic conflict), **material** (flat typography / documentary / instrument graphics), **motion** (static: this profile), **tone** (institutional, restrained, close, editorial, technical). When two axes conflict (very low density but ten things in the first screen), fix the task or form first; never squeeze by shrinking type.

## 7. Type and text [A7.1–A7.4]

Assign information roles first (title, body, numbers, captions, code), then faces: they need not all differ but must be distinguishable.

- Choose faces that fit language coverage, tone, reading distance and the rendering environment; confirm weights and actual character coverage (accented EN/IT/ES letters).
- Inter, Geist, a serif or a monospace is never the default answer. Do not mix families inside one title for fun; prefer weights, widths, italics and scale of one family.
- Body measure stays in the comfortable reading range.
- Key dates, sources, status, units and errors are never pale grey micro-text: their level may be low, their clarity may not.
- No remote fonts: legal local faces and a robust fallback chain.

Test with real content: the shortest, the longest, mixed accents, dense numbers. Body from 16 px and persistent secondary text from 14 px are starting points; any deviation is proven by reading distance, density and the final pixels together.

## 8. Colour [A8.1–A8.3]

Colour comes from evidence: the person's references, brand, the conventions of the chosen precedents, data meaning, the domain. Do not default to the safest option; the precedents' conventions decide light or dark ground and how many accents. A subject's own colour may enter objects and data, not automatically the page chrome.

- Give background, text, accent, links, state and data each a job that can be named.
- Build hierarchy with lightness, position, space and type first.
- Keep one warm or cool neutral logic; do not let elements bring different greys.
- Semantic colours appear only for their state and always with a non-colour redundancy.
- Shadows, textures, gradients and noise only when they express hierarchy or material.
- Check text and link contrast on the actual background.

**Colour role ledger.** In the representative frame, list each colour actually rendered: `token / value / where / job / non-colour redundancy / background and contrast / keep, merge, replace or delete`. Fail and fix when a colour has no job, one value carries conflicting meanings, one meaning drifts between values, a state or category is carried by colour alone, or contrast evidence is missing or fails. Regenerate the ledger after the last visual change.

## 9. Composition [A9.1–A9.3]

When composition is truly uncertain, sketch skeletons that reveal different trade-offs (in reading order and object relations, not just swapping left and right), then choose; when the medium has a clear structure and alternatives would not change the task, adopt it and record why. Check: the first point of attention is the most important content; block outlines read in the thumbnail; negative space makes pauses, not dead corners; evidence gets area proportional to its importance; the page does not break into too many containers; each target width re-plans relationships instead of scaling. A dominant signature expression is designed only when recognition, narrative or explanation really needs it, and the rest stays restrained.

## 10. Decision card [A10.1]

Before coding, record the fields that change implementation or acceptance: evidence and hypotheses (evidence ids, supported axes, protected invariants, gaps, `MODEL_HYPOTHESIS`); direction and mechanisms (the first visual, the minimal family, causal rules that become implementation or acceptance); the system (type, colour, density, rules and table treatment as a style contract); material (subject matter, transferable mechanisms, what must not be copied); expression (a signature only where the content needs one, or `N/A + reason`); what was deliberately dropped, and the risks the render must verify.

## 11. Argue against yourself before coding [A11.1]

1. Is a model hypothesis being treated as external evidence, or evidence crossing its supported axes?
2. Is a benchmark's look copied instead of its mechanism, or an element added that does no job?
3. Is the most prominent visual where the most important information is?
4. Are the person's preferences, protected invariants or domain constraints overridden by model taste?

Any "yes": change the decision card before coding.

**Excluded:** the component-library rules of §7 and §9 for product interfaces.
